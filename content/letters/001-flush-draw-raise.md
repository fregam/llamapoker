## The spot of the week

You hold **A♥ 5♥**. Two players see the flop with you, and it comes **K♥ 9♥ 2♣**. The first
player bets 50 into a pot of 100.

Call, raise or fold?

**The read: raise.** Nine hearts give you the best possible flush, and your ace sometimes wins
on its own. Against two players holding any two cards, that's about **54% equity**, your share
of the pot if the hand were played out many times. To call, you only need **25%**: you put in
50 to win a pot of 200. So calling is already profitable, and raising is better. A raise can win
the pot right now, and when you're called you still make your flush about 35% of the time by
the river. That's a *semi-bluff*: a bet that wins two ways.

One caveat: ranges matter. Against a tight player who only bets a strong king, your equity
drops. Put the spot on the table, change their range and watch the number move.

## The drill

**Count your outs, then use the rule of 2 and 4.** Outs are the cards that make your hand.
Multiply them by 4 on the flop, with two cards to come, or by 2 on the turn, with one card left,
and you have your chance of hitting as a quick percentage. Nine outs on the flop:
9 × 4 ≈ 36%. The exact figure is 35%, so the shortcut is close enough to decide with.
