# Hook playbook (Instagram, from 911 reels)

For the founder and for every writer agent. Method: `.scratch/hook-dissection/spec.md`.

**How the evidence was gathered:**
- **Sample:** 462 real outliers (≥ 100k views and ≥ 5× the account's median) from 132 accounts in
  many niches, each next to an ordinary reel from the same account posted around the same time.
- **Blind tagging:** agents tagged every opening without knowing which reels performed, using 29
  mechanisms; see [taxonomy.md](taxonomy.md).
- **Lift:** the share of each mechanism among outliers compared with ordinary reels; see
  [lift.md](lift.md).
- **Blind pair test:** fresh agents saw 120 outlier/ordinary pairs and guessed which was the hit.

## What the data says, plainly

1. **The opening explains only a small part of a hit.**
   - **Same video, different results:** one account posted the same video twice, 15 hours apart.
     One got 21,782 views and the other 507,813.
   - **Blind judges barely beat chance:** they picked the outlier 57.5% of the time, against 50%
     for a coin flip. When they felt confident they were right 7 times out of 8; when they felt
     unsure they were at chance.
   - **What this means:** most pairs are indistinguishable from the opening alone. A good hook
     raises the odds; it can't guarantee reach.

2. **No move is magic, including the ones writers love.**
   - Specific numbers, mid-action starts, counterintuitive claims, open questions and withheld
     payoffs all appear as often in a creator's ordinary reels as in their hits (lift ≈ 1).
   - A number-first hook is not a hook strategy. That is why the first test's stat-led lines read
     flat.

3. **What did separate hits from ordinary reels:**
   - **Who the opening is for: a stranger.**
     - This was the clearest pattern in the confident correct picks of the blind test.
     - Hits opened on something anyone can feel in one second: an emotional moment, a relatable
       type ("the guy at your company who does nothing"), a conflict, money, a famous person, a
       wedding or a loss.
     - Ordinary reels were aimed at people who already follow: ads and sponsorships, event promos,
       "round 3" of a series, weekly roundups, tips for a niche ("for coaches growing on
       Instagram").
     - In the lift table this shows up three ways: fewer list/how-to promises in hits (lift 0.71),
       more raw disclosure (3.0), and more famous faces (1.35; 1.62 in our format).
   - **Something happens.**
     - Reels with no cut in the first 5 s are less common among hits (0.89).
     - Plain to-camera talking is less common among hits (0.85), and so are interview or stage
       clips (0.52).
     - In our format (voiceover or to-camera), at least one cut by 5 s goes with hits (1.32).
     - This is a tilt, not a law: visual spectacle alone lost several pairs (an explosion, a
       smashed barrel, a glowing coil).
   - **More than one move at once.** Openings carrying 3 or more moves are a little more common
     among hits (1.26). The moves have to land together, not be stacked as a list.

## What this means for our hooks

The writer's job: **in one line, give a poker regular who doesn't follow us a reason to care,
right now.** The moves below are tools for that, not boxes to tick.

- **Aim at the stranger, not the student.**
  - "Here are 3 preflop tips" is a lesson for someone who already wants lessons. It's the format
    that underperforms.
  - Open on the feeling the lesson is about: the hand you lost, the call you regret, the thing
    every reg at the table believes. Then let the lesson be the payoff.
- **Make the first line a scene or a stake, not a fact.** A number can live inside the line ("I
  folded aces 3 times last night and I'd do it again"). It shouldn't be the whole hook.
- **Name the viewer's own experience.** A relatable type or moment ("the guy who limps every hand
  and somehow wins") gets tagged and shared. A taxonomy of poker theory doesn't.
- **Disagree with the table, not with physics.** A counterintuitive claim earns its place when it
  contradicts what the viewer does every session, not a trivia fact.
- **Plan the cut.** For a narrated reel, the picture should change by second 1–3 on the key word
  (see `instant_cutaway` and `cuts_5s` in lift.md).
- **Never open like an ad, a promo or an episode.** No "today we're going to", "part 2", or
  product-first openings.

**Where the judges were wrong** (the hit was the "weaker" opening): explosive visuals lost to plain

question lost to a niche business story. The opening is a lever, not the whole machine.

## Limits

- **Correlation, not cause.** Topic, timing and the algorithm's own variance aren't controlled.
- **Not poker-specific.** The sample spans niches and has few poker outliers. Our own posted reels
  are the real test, so measure retention at 3 s once they are live.
- **Unchecked moves.** Rare mechanisms got too few double-tags to check agreement; lift.md marks
  them "unverified".
- **Proposed mechanisms.** Six new ones were proposed while tagging (pre-empting the sceptic,
  coined term, hypothetical premise, viewer participation). None recurred more than twice, so they
  are logged, not added.
