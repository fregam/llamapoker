# Why @ziramium_poker works

**The account in one line:** a faceless poker-education account with **13.8K followers from 45 posts**
in roughly three months. Every post is a 40–60 second animated explainer Reel. Bio: "Complex poker
concepts explained simply. Not just what to do, but how and why it works." It's almost exactly our
positioning, so it's the closest proof that the LlamaPoker idea works on Instagram.

## Why it works

**1. The topics are habits every casual player has, framed as a question.** The four hits, "Waiting for
aces?", "Limp", "Outs in percent" and "Push-fold", are things a beginner *does* or *wonders about* at every
session. The flops are off-table or abstract topics, like bankroll, player types, tournament review,
"whose board is it", dead outs and solvers. The broad rule: **"you're doing this, here's what it costs
you"** beats **"here's a concept"**.

**2. Each video is about one idea, and proves it with numbers on screen.** "Waiting for aces" shows:
premium hands are **3% of all hands**, then a counter, **"waiting 33 hands"**, while "the blinds keep
coming", and then why the big hand doesn't get paid. A visible number makes the point undeniable, and
viewers save and share it.

**3. They have a hook and tension in the first 3 seconds.** "The plan seems rock solid" → "But there are
2 problems". The viewer is told they're wrong before they know why. That's what stops the scroll.

**4. It's a recognisable series.** The grid is a wall of matching covers: a giant condensed headline
(one question, two or three words) over a vintage screen-print illustration with one visual metaphor
(a padlock for "weak ace", an hourglass of chips for "waiting for aces", a polar bear for "linear or
polar"). People who see two posts recognise the third, and a profile visit looks like a curriculum,
which makes them follow.

**5. Inside, the videos are clean motion graphics, not AI video.** Flat cards, the 13×13 range grid
lighting up, counters and bars, plus word-by-word captions with the key word highlighted, over a calm
voiceover. Easy to watch on mute, readable on a phone, and the visuals carry the teaching. One commenter
said it outright: "Thank you for the visual aids… easy on the eyes."

**6. Shares drive the algorithm.** The hits get about **1 share for every 4 likes**, which is very high.
Poker players send these to the friend who limps or the friend who only plays aces ("if you can't find
the nit at the table, it's probably you": 812 likes on that comment). Instagram pushes Reels that get
sent in DMs.

**7. The comments argue.** "Limp" and "Waiting for aces" pull "I'd rather limp" and "nits are going to be
so pressed" replies. Mild controversy plus a clear answer brings comments, then reach.

**8. The captions changed.** Early captions were hashtag lists ("#Poker #BankrollManagement…"). The hits
use two plain sentences: what the video explains, then **who it's for** ("For players who play very tight
and wonder why their big hands never get paid"). The second line makes the right viewer feel seen.

**9. They post often.** 45 posts in about 3 months is roughly 3–4 a week. The first ~15 posts got 1–4K
views each. It only took off around the 16th post, when the topics shifted to everyday habits.

## How their hits are cut (frame-by-frame, 2026-11-11)

Stepped through "Waiting for aces?" (322K views, **112 s**), "Limp" (286K, 102 s), "Outs in percent"
(208K, 50 s) and "Your draw backs the bluff" (51K, 75 s), one frame every 3 s. The length isn't the
trick, the grammar is:

1. **The voice leads, the screen follows.** The caption pill at the bottom is the phrase being
   spoken right now: 2–5 words, one in yellow, changing every 1.5–2.5 s. Every new phrase brings
   exactly one change on screen (a cell lights, a seat appears, a number ticks). ~1.7–2.3 words/s.
2. **One object at a time, centred, big.** Two cards fill 40% of the width; the 13×13 grid fills
   the whole width with every cell labelled; a table seen from above with six seat pills. The rest
   is empty teal. Never two focal things at once.
3. **A scene evolves instead of being replaced.** Grid → the premiums light → "everything else
   away" dims the rest → "3% OF ALL HANDS" attaches top-left → later the same grid comes back with
   "YOU PLAY 3%", toggles EARLY/BUTTON, widens to 19% then 43%. Numbers accumulate as a HUD
   (WAITING 33 HANDS progress bar, PAID IN BLINDS +7.5 BB with chip icons per orbit).
4. **The headline exists only in the hook** ("ONLY TOP HANDS / WHY DOES IT LOSE?" over K♠K♥ for
   about 3 s). After that there are no titles: caption plus one graphic.
5. **No character, no illustration inside the video.** The vintage art is on the cover only.
6. **Ending:** the same cards as the hook come back, so the loop lands on the hook.

What we take: the caption/stage sync (our scripts carry cues per word, `apps/reels/src/narration.ts`),
one change per phrase, one focal object, evolving scenes, headline only in the hook. What we keep
of ours: typeset captions instead of the pill, the Gentleman as a bookend, a save-worthy table,
the URL, and an explicit comment question.

## Where they're weak (our opening)

- **No link and no product.** There's no website, tool or newsletter in the bio, so all that attention
  goes nowhere. We have a free tool and a newsletter, so every Reel can say "run this spot on
  llamapoker.com".
- **No face or character.** It's purely informational. The Gentleman gives us a personality they don't
  have, which is better for merch and for being remembered.
- **Advanced topics underperform** (linear/polar, solver, capped range), yet they keep making them. Our
  audience is beginners, which is exactly the segment that made their four hits.

## What we do with this

1. **Format:** vertical explainer Reels of 60–100 seconds (their hits run 50–112 s). One idea each,
   with a hook in the first line, numbers on screen and voice-synced typeset captions in the brand
   style (ink, cream, Bodoni headline, Inter captions, sage/brass/vermilion only for meaning). The
   Gentleman appears in the opening and closing frames only.
2. **Make the videos from code, not AI video.** We already have the poker engine and the site's design
   system. A small video renderer in the repo (for example Remotion, React-based) can turn a spot into
   an animated Reel with **exact numbers from our own maths**. It's cheap, repeatable and always on
   brand. Use Higgsfield for the cover illustrations (the Gentleman in a scene for each topic) and,
   optionally, the voiceover.
3. **Covers:** the same idea as theirs, but ours. A giant Bodoni headline (one question) over a Gentleman
   engraving scene with one metaphor, so the grid reads as a series.
4. **Topics first, the habits beginners have** (straight from our Learn guides):
   - "Only playing aces?" (tight is not winning)
   - "Stop limping" (why raising first beats calling)
   - "Your outs in 2 seconds" (rule of 2 and 4, then a link to /learn/outs)
   - "Should you call?" (pot odds with one formula, then /learn/pot-odds)
   - "The button is the best seat" (positions)
   - "AK isn't a made hand" (overvaluing AK)
   - "The flush draw that wants to be raised" (Letter No. 1 as a Reel)
   - "Calling from the big blind" (our new BB-defence chart)
5. **Captions:** two sentences: what it explains, then who it's for. Then "Run any spot free: link in bio".
   Few or no hashtags.
6. **Design for shares:** end on a one-screen cheat sheet worth saving ("4 draws, 4 numbers"), and frame
   topics so people want to send them to a friend.
7. **Cadence:** 3 a week, and expect the first 10–15 to be slow. They needed about 15 posts before one
   broke out.
