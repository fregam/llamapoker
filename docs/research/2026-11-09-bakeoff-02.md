# Bake-off 02

**Shot:** same as bake-off 01. The Gentleman at the card table leans back, straightens his
bow tie, then turns and looks into the lens. 5 s, vertical.

**New input:** keyframes made from the permanent image library (`content/library/README.md`)
with GPT Image 2.5, instead of a frame lifted from Reel 001. Prompts describe hooves only
positively and never name fingers or hands.

| | Approach | Cost | Style held | Motion | Hooves | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Seedance 2.5 **Draft**, 480p, from bake-off 01's keyframe | 156 cr ≈ $0.73 | ✗ smooth painterly | ✓ right beats | ✗ hand-like | Usable as a **motion draft** only |
| B1 | **Kling 3.0 first+last frame** (library keyframes) | 133 cr ≈ $0.62 | ✓ | ✓ lands exactly on the end frame | ~ soft at the bow tie | Good; camera drifts and stray chips appear mid-shot |
| B2 | **Seedance 2.0 first+last frame** | ≈ 230 cr ≈ $1.10 | ✓✓ best texture | ✗ hidden jump cut, looks at the lens too early | ✓ | Beautiful frames, unreliable motion |
| C | **Wan Animate 2** re-skin: motion from A, look from the start keyframe | GPU time only (cents) | ✓ | ✓ copies the driving motion exactly | ✗ copies the driver's hands | Works once the driving clip has hooves and we have the LoRA |
| E | **Kling 4.0 image-to-video** (start keyframe only, timed beats in the prompt) | 237 cr ≈ $1.11 | ✓ | ✓✓ hits every timed beat, then a slow push-in | ✓ | **Winner** |

## What we learned

1. **The library is what made the difference.** Every clip animated from a library keyframe kept the
   engraving style. The bake-off 01 frame and the draft lost it.
2. **Kling 4.0 follows timecoded direction** ("0–1.5 s … 1.5–3.5 s … 3.5–5 s …"), so the shot list's
   timings can be written straight into the prompt. That's how motion stops feeling isolated:
   every beat lands where the voice-over needs it.
3. **Motion transfer (Wan Animate) is precise but literal.** It copies whatever limbs the driver
   has. The plan is to generate drivers in which the Gentleman already has hooves (a Kling 4.0
   or Seedance draft from library keyframes), or to train the LoRA, then use Wan Animate for cheap,
   exact re-takes and for 1080p.
4. **First/last frame** is the tool for chaining shots (the end frame of one shot is the start of
   the next). Kling 3.0 holds the motion better; Seedance 2.0 holds the texture better.

## Decisions (2026-11-09)

- **Seedance is dropped from the pipeline** (all variants). It is the most expensive per second,
  and its one strength (texture) doesn't make up for its unreliable motion.
- **Motion is authored in Blender** (see `2026-11-09-blender-motion.md`): exact timing and camera,
  rendered as control passes, then pushed through our workflow.

## Routing (v2)

| Shot type | Model | Approx. cost / 5 s |
|---|---|---|
| Character acting, prompt-directed | **Kling 4.0 i2v**, library keyframe + timecoded prompt | $1.11 |
| Shot that must end on an exact pose or chain into the next | Kling 3.0 first+last frame | $0.62 |
| Motion authored in Blender (exact timing and camera) | Wan 2.2 / LTX-2.3 with depth/pose control + library keyframe (bake-off 03) | cents |
| Sets, props, inserts without the character | LTX-2.5 (open) | cents |
| Every shot, final pass | SeedVR2 → 1080×1920 | cents |
